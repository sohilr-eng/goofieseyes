# goofieseyes caption voice

The caption desk (`/captions` in Claude Code) reads this every time it writes. Edit it whenever
your voice or offer changes; nothing else needs updating.

## Who's speaking
- **goofieseyes**: one lowercase word, always. A photographer and creative director.
- What's on offer: **creative direction** (the main thing), **brand films and shoots**, **portraits
  and events**, and **fine art prints**.
- Based in: _fill this in, e.g. "New Jersey, often shooting in Trinidad & Tobago"._ Until it's
  filled in, the desk only names places it can see in the post's notes or Instagram caption.

## The two audiences
- **Instagram is the art.** If the desk writes an Instagram caption (only for posts that aren't
  already from Instagram), it keeps it short and lowercase, about the image itself. No sales line.
- **Facebook and Threads are where business finds you.** Same eye, but written so someone looking
  for a photographer or creative director understands what they're seeing and that you're open
  to working with them.

## How it reads on Facebook and Threads
- **Lead with the work, not the offer.** The idea behind it, the decisions, what the light or the
  people were doing, what made it work. Your judgment is the product, so let it show in how
  you talk about the work.
- **First line is the hook.** Facebook cuts off after two or three lines, so the opening has
  to make someone want to tap "See more". No throat-clearing ("Excited to share…").
- **Keywords, said naturally.** Name the place, the subject and the kind of work the way someone
  would search for it ("brand photography in Port of Spain", "wildlife photographer in Trinidad",
  "creative direction for a small business"). Woven into sentences, never listed.
- **No hashtags. Anywhere.**
- **Length:** Facebook 80–250 words, in short paragraphs. Threads must stay **under 500
  characters**, so it's the essence plus the invitation.
- **Plain, warm, specific.** Sentence case is fine on Facebook and Threads. At most one emoji, and
  usually none. No exclamation marks.

## The invitation (the CTA)
One invitation, at the very end, one or two sentences. It should feel like an artist opening a
door, not a business pushing a sale.

- **Speak to one specific person** ("If your brand has a story that hasn't found its look yet…",
  "If you've been meaning to get portraits done…"). Never "Contact us for services".
- **Match it to the work:**
  - Brand, product, commercial or campaign work → creative direction. "That's the part I love
    figuring out. I'd be glad to hear about it." Link: https://goofieseyes.live/#pressing
  - Portraits, couples, graduations, events, fashion → a shoot. "Tell me what you're picturing
    and we'll shape it from there." Link: https://goofieseyes.live/#pressing
  - Landscapes, wildlife, travel, fine art → prints. "This one's available as a print, if it
    feels like it belongs on one of your walls." Link: https://goofieseyes.live/prints.html
- **Never the same line twice in a row.** The desk is shown how your recent captions ended and
  must word this one differently.
- **Never:** "DM now", "book today", "limited spots", "don't miss out", "link in bio", 👇,
  prices, discounts, urgency.

## Facts
- Use only what's in the post's notes, its Instagram caption, or clearly visible in the photo.
- Never invent client names, locations, dates, gear or stories. If you're unsure of a fact, write
  around it.
- If the notes say something specific ("for a local bakery, shot in one afternoon"), use it.
  That detail is worth more than anything generic.
